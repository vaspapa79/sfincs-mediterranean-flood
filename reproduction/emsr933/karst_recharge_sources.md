# Cretan karst recharge coefficients — literature record for the "karst bracket" sensitivity (GO-C decision 6c)

Compiled 2026-10-19 by a web-research agent of the Claude Code session (sources read in full text unless marked);
kept verbatim as the provenance record for `rule_ledger.md` §8. All values are ANNUAL recharge fractions
(fraction of mean annual precipitation) unless flagged. Nothing here was tuned to the event.

## 1. Primary — independently derived, verbatim, open access
**Steiakakis, E. (2018).** Evaluation of Exploitable Groundwater Reserves in Karst Terrain: A Case Study from Crete,
Greece. *Geosciences* 8(1), 19. https://doi.org/10.3390/geosciences8010019 (PDF read, p. 4–5).
Verbatim: "Due to the lack of meteorological data for estimation of evapotranspiration in the region, the coefficient
of effective infiltration and consequently the amount of the annual recharge of the karst system was determined by
hydrologic balance calculations [25]. The annual yield of the springs was estimated around 44.7 × 10⁶ m³, the
secondary recharge was evaluated equal to 8.1 × 10⁶ m³/y, and the "estimated" direct infiltration (i.e. the
difference between springs yield and secondary recharge) amounts in 36.6 × 10⁶ m³/y. Taking into account that the
aquifer is constrained by well-defined permeability boundaries, the quantity of direct infiltration was compared to
average annual rainfall in the hydrogeological basin (71.32 × 10⁶ m³/y), and the **infiltration coefficient for the
carbonate rocks of the basin was estimated equal to 51.3%**."
Area: Kourtaliotis spring catchment (Kedros–Kryoneritis–Kouroupa–Asideroto carbonate massif, Tripolis-unit +
Plattenkalk limestones), central-southern Crete (Rethymno) — a Cretan karst massif, not Psiloritis/Dikti/Lefka Ori.
Method: annual water balance closed on measured spring discharge (≈1.4 m³/s) vs isohyet-derived precipitation,
1967–1987. Original derivation: Steiakakis, Monopolis, Vavadakis & Lambrakis (2011), in *Advances in the Research of
Aquatic Environment* vol. 1, Springer, 485–492, https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-19902-8_57 (paywalled; number not
verified there directly).

## 2. Secondary — massif-specific spread
- **Dikti** — Voudouris, Alexopoulos, Antonakos & Kallergis (2007). Water resources in the wider area of the
  Aposelemis basin, Crete island, Greece. *Bull. Geol. Soc. Greece* 40(2), 616–628. https://doi.org/10.12681/bgsg.16342
  (read). Verbatim: "The infiltration coefficient is high of the order of 50 %." / "The percentage of annual
  precipitation infiltrating into the carbonates is estimated to be around 50 %." Table 2: rest of the Dikti drainage
  area — rainfall 470.0 × 10⁶ m³ (1041 mm), ET 38.4 %, infiltration 260.6 × 10⁶ m³ (55.4 %); Lasithi plateau 45.4 %;
  Aposelemis basin 26.8 %. Method: Thornthwaite–Mather + literature coefficients (Voudouris 2006). ANNUAL.
- **Lefka Ori (Sfakia)** — Tzanakakis et al. (2022). Uncoupled precipitation and water availability: the case study of
  Municipality of Sfakia, Crete, Greece. *Water* 14(3), 462. https://doi.org/10.3390/w14030462 (read, p. 7, 13).
  Verbatim: "The recharge coefficient has been estimated equal to 54%, due to the karstic nature of the geological
  relief." (from the Crete River Basin Management Plan, CMD 896/2017). ANNUAL.
- **Lefka Ori N foothills (Agyia, Trypali unit)** — Steiakakis, Vavadakis & Mourkakou (2023). *Water* 15(2), 231.
  https://doi.org/10.3390/w15020231 (read, p. 6). "55% of the precipitation infiltrates … surface run off is very
  limited (about 5%)" citing Lionis & Perleros (2001, Ministry of Agriculture, in Greek). ANNUAL, secondary citation.
- **Island-wide official table** — Perleros, Papamastorakis, Kritsotakis, Drakopoulou & Panagopoulos (2004).
  Groundwater potential of the island of Crete. *Bull. Geol. Soc. Greece* 36(4), 2048–2056.
  https://doi.org/10.12681/bgsg.16704 (read, Greek). Table 1 infiltration coefficients: karst formations of high to
  moderate permeability 55 % (west) – 50 % (east); moderate to low permeability 45 % (west) – 40 % (east); set from
  karst-spring discharge records (Region of Crete / IGME). ANNUAL.
- Context (non-karst land included): Malagò et al. (2016) *J. Hydrol.* 540, 64–81 (55 % percolation island-wide);
  Tzanakakis et al. (2020) *Water* 12, 1538, Table 8: karstic formations 3333 km², mean percolation 42.55 %. ANNUAL.

## 3. Scale flag
All of the above are ANNUAL fractions. Event-scale counter-evidence on the same Lefka Ori karst: Lilli et al. (2020)
*Water* 12(9), 2474, https://doi.org/10.3390/w12092474 (Koiliaris CZO, 5-day 400-mm event): "70% of the rainfall
becomes surface runoff", 10 % spring flow, 0.7 % ET during the event; "when the infiltration capacity of the karst is
exceeded by precipitation, the potential for significant and catastrophic floods is certain." A constant rate derived
from a ~0.5 annual coefficient is therefore an UPPER-bound infiltration scenario for the event.
